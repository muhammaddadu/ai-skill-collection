# Creative review rubric

Use this rubric after every meaningful visual round. Review each concept independently before comparing routes.

## Review record

### What works

Identify 2–5 specific strengths. Refer to observable choices—form, spacing, type, colour, imagery, composition, behaviour—not general approval.

### What does not work yet

Identify 2–5 specific weaknesses. Distinguish a fixable execution issue from a weak underlying idea.

### Strategic fit

- Which brand characteristics does the concept reinforce?
- Which does it undermine or ignore?
- Does it express the brand promise and productive personality tensions?
- Is the rationale visible in the design, or only in the explanation?

### Distinctiveness

- Does it escape the category's obvious conventions?
- Is it recognisable without relying on the name?
- Is the idea ownable as a system rather than a single mark?
- Does it feel derived from a known competitor, studio, campaign, or trend?

### Memorability

- Can someone describe the core visual idea after a brief exposure?
- Is there one dominant idea, or several competing gestures?
- Does simplification strengthen or erase the concept?

### Practicality

Test rather than assume:

- tiny favicon/app-icon and compact header
- monochrome and one-colour reproduction
- light and dark backgrounds
- large signage or environmental scale
- formal print/document use
- responsive/digital layouts
- legibility and accessible contrast
- realistic production constraints

### System potential

- Can typography, colour, imagery, layout, motion, and voice carry the identity when the logo is absent?
- Can the concept generate varied applications without becoming repetitive?
- Does the route have enough range for formal and expressive contexts?

### Risk

- Is the symbol or name likely to be confused with another brand?
- Are cultural interpretations or accessibility issues unresolved?
- Does the concept depend on licensed fonts, imagery, or production methods that are unavailable?
- Is a preliminary trademark or domain screen being mistaken for clearance?

### Next move

Specify exact changes for the next round. Examples:

- preserve the mark's counterform, but remove the fragile outer stroke
- replace the category-standard blue with the tested high-contrast accent route
- retain the editorial type hierarchy while killing the generic monogram
- test the symbol at 16 px before developing more mockups

Do not end with “explore further.” Name what to keep, change, test, or kill.

## Route comparison

After individual reviews, compare surviving routes against the same criteria:

1. strategic fit
2. distinctiveness
3. memorability
4. system potential
5. practical resilience
6. accessibility
7. production realism
8. longevity

Use evidence from rendered tests and applications. Do not turn the comparison into an unweighted score that hides judgment. State the recommended route, the decisive reasons, the largest remaining risk, and the next proof required.
