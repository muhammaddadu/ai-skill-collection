# Failure modes and anti-default review

Read this before research or visual exploration. These are warning signs, not automatic bans. A familiar device may survive only when it is transformed enough to become ownable and the rest of the system carries recognition.

## Quality hierarchy

When trade-offs are unavoidable, optimise in this order:

1. strength of the core idea
2. distinctiveness and memorability
3. typographic and formal craft
4. capacity to become a rich visual system
5. strategic fit
6. practical performance
7. application quality
8. presentation quality
9. documentation completeness

A polished deck, atmospheric mockup, or articulate rationale cannot rescue an average identity.

## Common category defaults

Flag and deliberately challenge:

- literal house, key, route, map pin, or tick for property and moving
- shield, handshake, scales, or gavel for trust, legal, or professional services
- leaf for sustainability
- spark or asterisk for AI and technology
- abstract gradient blob presented as innovation
- rounded-square app icon used as the primary identity idea
- dark green, cream, and gold used as shorthand for premium
- generic high-contrast serif used as shorthand for heritage
- pastel colour and rounded sans used as shorthand for friendly
- blue used as shorthand for trust
- thin decorative lines without conceptual purpose
- generic editorial serif plus neutral sans chosen before alternatives are tested
- stock-photo emotion compensating for a weak visual system

## Contradiction check

Maintain `cliche-red-list.md` with category symbols, palette defaults, typography defaults, verbal and mockup clichés, and specific resemblance risks.

If research identifies a convention to avoid, a later route may not quietly reintroduce it under a different rationale. Record the contradiction and return to exploration.

## Final red-team questions

- What is the identity's most generic element?
- What would a senior identity designer challenge immediately?
- Which decision is taste without strategic or formal support?
- Does the work look designed or generated?
- Is typography doing enough?
- Is there a memorable visual idea beyond the mark?
- Does the rationale exceed the visible design quality?
- Is the palette suspiciously predictable for the category?
- Would the system survive without hero mockups?
- What could be removed to make it stronger?

Fix serious weaknesses before presentation or handover.
