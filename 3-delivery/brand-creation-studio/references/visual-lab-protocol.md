# Visual lab protocol

Use this during divergent exploration and route development. The goal is visual evidence, not a large collection of prompts or polished moodboards.

## Independent rounds

Explore at least four conceptual families. When project scope supports it, aim for 40–80 raw ideas across several independent rounds rather than one giant generated sketch sheet.

Useful families include pure wordmarks, typographic interruptions or ligatures, monograms, negative-space constructions, abstract or geometric devices, transformed metaphors, type-and-layout-only systems, responsive marks, and image-led identities with a quiet logo.

After every generated round:

1. inspect what was actually rendered
2. identify accidental clichés and resemblance risks
3. identify promising formal ideas, not merely promising rationales
4. reject visually weak outputs even when their prompts sounded intelligent
5. base the next round on those observations

Never critique a prompt in place of inspecting the image. Record decisions in `decision-log.md`.

## Mark proof

Promising marks must work in black and white before colour is allowed to help them. Test silhouette, memorability, category resemblance, dependence on effects, and whether one dominant idea survives.

Treat generated logo geometry as a sketch. Redraw viable forms in SVG or another deterministic design method, then test optical balance, counterspace, stroke consistency, corners, proportion, positive/negative form, one-colour use, 16/24/48 px, and horizontal and stacked lockups.

## Exact typography

Final typography exploration uses real named typefaces and accurate text, never generated lettering. Where relevant, produce 10–20 exact studies varying classification, case, width, weight, tracking, line breaks, punctuation, optical relationships, and selective custom modifications.

Record typeface names, sources, and licensing status. Make each typeface earn its role; do not default to a fashionable serif/sans pairing.

## System probes before selection

Before a route can be shortlisted, prove that it can create more than a logo. Build a compact flat system probe containing:

- exact logo or wordmark
- type hierarchy
- primary colour behaviour
- a recognisable composition or layout device
- a graphic device, pattern, frame, or crop logic
- imagery direction
- one flat digital application
- one flat print or environmental application

Flat comps precede glossy mockups. A route advances on visible system evidence, not on a territory description.
